# Findings: all Wait Watcher data (RTT, DM01, Staff Survey, Friends and Family, CQC), August 2026

## What we've got
- **Treatment waits (RTT):** 29 months, April 2024 to August 2026, 566 providers. England, August 2026: 7.19 million waiting, 65.2% within 18 weeks, 112,958 over a year. These match the published figures and last month's findings, which already cover trusts, treatments, areas and year-long waits, so this pass only looks for new angles.
- **Test waits (DM01):** 15 tests, same 29 months, 484 providers. England, August 2026: 1.83 million waiting, 24.7% for 6+ weeks (the standard is under 1%), up from 24.0% a year ago, while the queue grew 9%.
- **Staff Survey:** 2025 only, 238 organisations, 121 of them acute trusts. Typical acute trust: 61% of staff would be happy for a friend or relative to be treated there.
- **Friends and Family:** 29 months, about 22 million responses. Steady for two years: inpatients about 95% rate their care good or very good, outpatients 94%, A&E 78%.
- **CQC:** 107 trusts with an overall rating: 52 Requires improvement, 47 Good, 8 Outstanding.
- **Joined up:** 113 acute trusts with 10,000+ waiting, matched across all five sources.

Expectation written down first: "trusts where staff are unhappy will have much longer waits and unhappier patients." **The data mostly disagreed.** Staff views and waits move together only weakly. The best quarter of trusts for staff views have 67.5% within 18 weeks, against 64.0% for the worst quarter. Patient ratings barely move with waits at all. The interesting stories are elsewhere.

## What's odd (and what we did about it)
- **March 2026:** England jumped 2.7 points in one month (62.5% to 65.2%), right on the government's "65% by March 2026" milestone. The list shrank by 110,702 that month. *Story (finding 2), not a data error.*
- **One Friends and Family row at 101.3% positive** (provider R1K, April 2025). *Excluded.* Rows flagged small numbers (20% of inpatient rows) are also excluded.
- **CQC ratings stop at August 2024.** Nothing newer is in the October 2026 file. *Story (finding 6), but check on the CQC website before publishing.*
- **27 big acute trusts have no CQC rating in the kitchen**, including Manchester University and Mid and South Essex. Most are trusts formed by mergers. *Excluded from rating comparisons.* This may be a kitchen matching gap rather than truly unrated trusts, so check before saying "unrated".
- **ICB staff (area boards) score 41% on "happy for a relative to be treated here"**, but area boards don't treat anyone. *Excluded.*
- **Specialist hospitals top every staff league** (Christie, Royal Marsden, Robert Jones and Agnes Hunt, all about 90%). *Excluded from comparisons with general hospitals: not like with like.*
- **Staff Survey is one year only.** *No trend claims possible.*

## Findings, ranked

**1. In Lincolnshire, waits for treatment got better this year. Waits for tests got much worse.**
- The numbers: United Lincolnshire Teaching Hospitals, August 2025 to August 2026. Treatment within 18 weeks went from 53.6% to 63.0% (+9.4 points, one of England's bigger improvements). Over the same year, people waiting 6+ weeks for a test went from 5,615 to 20,177, and the share from 29% to 58%. By test: heart scans (echo) 15% to 73%, ultrasound 14% to 61%, MRI 17% to 50%, bone-density scans 1% to 38%. Hearing tests stayed bad (89% to 83%). Colonoscopy and CT stayed fine (3% and 1%). Staff: 43% would be happy for a relative to be treated there, fourth lowest in England.
- The picture: before/after bars, two pairs: "treatment waits" (improved) and "test waits" (worse).
- Strength: strong. Both changes are steady month on month.
- Watch out: correlation, not a trade-off. We can't say one caused the other. The scans brief already uses Lincolnshire twice, so this is the one to keep and the others drop, or it reads as a campaign against one trust.

**2. The NHS hit its March target. Since then, year-long waits have crept back up by about 17,000.**
- The numbers: England, people waiting over a year: 124,280 (February 2026), 95,824 (March), 112,958 (August). The waiting list shrank by 110,702 in March and has grown by 143,703 since. The typical big trust improved 2.4 points in March 2026 alone, against 0.6 points in March 2025. In University Hospitals Sussex, year-long waits are back to exactly where they were a year ago (5,634 against 5,629). In County Durham and Darlington they've tripled since March (415 to 1,410).
- The picture: three bars (February, March, August), with March in green.
- Strength: strong.
- Watch out: it's still down 41% on a year ago, so lead with that. A push before a deadline (extra clinics, checking lists for people who no longer need treatment) is normal management, not proof of gaming. Don't imply wrongdoing.

**3. Nerve tests in Birmingham: at one hospital trust, 9 in 10 have waited over 6 weeks. At the other, 1 in 200.**
- The numbers: August 2026, nerve conduction tests (for example, for carpal tunnel). University Hospitals Birmingham: 4,623 of 5,242 (88%) waiting 6+ weeks, and 3,490 over 13 weeks. Sandwell and West Birmingham: 2 of 380. Coventry and Warwickshire, next door: 48 of 975 (5%).
- The picture: 100 people, two rows.
- Strength: medium. University Hospitals Birmingham is a regional centre with 14 times the queue, so harder cases are likely.
- Watch out: "peripheral neurophysiology" needs translating ("nerve tests"). Treat it as a single-test postcode lottery, not a hospital ranking.

**4. More people are waiting for a colonoscopy, and fewer are being done.**
- The numbers: England, August 2024 to August 2026. People waiting: 60,151 to 76,972 (+28%). Colonoscopies done in August: 56,916 to 55,229 (−3%). Waiting 6+ weeks: 27% to 31%, about 1 in 3. Over 13 weeks: 7,795 to 10,454. The spread runs from Portsmouth, where 78% have waited 6+ weeks (788 of 1,006), to Mid Yorkshire, where it's 2 of 552.
- The picture: two bars (queue then and now), or a big number ("+28%").
- Strength: strong nationally; the place element is medium (Portsmouth and Mid Yorkshire aren't neighbours).
- Watch out: DM01 mixes urgent and routine colonoscopies, and cancer waits aren't in the kitchen. Don't say "cancer checks are delayed". Gastroscopy is the same shape (queue +27%, scopes done −6%).

**5. Good news: waits for heart scans, hearing tests and CT scans are coming down.**
- The numbers: England, August 2025 to August 2026. People waiting over 3 months for a hearing test: 33,783 to 27,192 (−20%). Heart scans: 6+ week waits 50,129 to 46,911, with 3.5% more done. CT: over 3 months 6,895 to 4,690 (−32%). North-east London heart scans went from 12% waiting 6+ weeks to 1% (36 of 3,594).
- The picture: before/after bars, three tests.
- Strength: strong.
- Watch out: all three are still far from the under-1% standard (hearing tests 43%, heart scans 30%, CT 11%).

**6. The typical hospital's inspection rating is over 6 years old.**
- The numbers: across 107 trusts with a CQC overall rating, the median age is 6.6 years. 64% are over 5 years old, 91% over 3 years. The newest is from August 2024. The "Good" ratings are older (median 7.2 years) than the "Requires improvement" ones (4.5 years).
- The picture: ladder or big number ("6 years").
- Strength: medium. Strong if the CQC website confirms there are no newer overall ratings than our file shows.
- Watch out: newer inspections may rate individual services without changing the trust's overall rating. "Good ratings are older" may just reflect that CQC goes back where it's worried. Don't say "recent inspections downgrade".

**7. Where staff would least want their family treated, patients wait longer for tests.**
- The numbers: 113 acute trusts split into quarters by "happy for a friend or relative to be treated here". Bottom quarter (typical 49%) against top quarter (74%): 26 against 19 in 100 people waiting 6+ weeks for a test, 64.0% against 67.5% treated within 18 weeks, and A&E rated good by 76% against 81%. The lowest: King's Lynn 35%, Mid and South Essex 38%, Princess Alexandra 43%, Lincolnshire 43%. Neighbours of King's Lynn: Norfolk and Norwich 50%, James Paget 61%.
- The picture: two bars (top and bottom quarter, test waits).
- Strength: thin-to-medium. The links are weak (a correlation of about 0.3), and one survey year is a snapshot.
- Watch out: correlation, not cause, both ways. This is the finding most likely to be read as "blame the staff". Point at systems, never at people.

**8. Sleep-test queues grew by half in a year.**
- The numbers: England, people waiting for a sleep study: 28,466 (August 2025) to 42,276 (August 2026), +49%, while 11% more studies were done. Leicestershire went from 16% waiting 6+ weeks to 70%. South Yorkshire went from 18% to 60%.
- The picture: two bars.
- Strength: medium. A real rise, but there's no explanation in the data.
- Watch out: it's tempting to guess why (sleep apnoea, weight-loss drugs). Don't.

## What didn't make the cut
- **Patient ratings vs waits.** No link (correlation about 0.1). True, but "nothing happened" doesn't make a post.
- **Patients rate care 95%, staff 61%.** Different questions asked of different people. Homework.
- **A&E rated lower than wards (78% vs 95%).** Well known, steady for two years, no place.
- **"Outstanding" trusts have shorter waits.** Only 3 rated Outstanding among the 113. Small numbers.
- **Specialist hospitals' staff are happiest.** Not like with like.
- **University Hospitals Sussex: no progress on year-long waits in a year.** True and striking, but too close to naming and shaming. It stays as a supporting line in finding 2.
- **Bladder tests (urodynamics), 47% over 6 weeks.** Small queue (9,901), needs explaining.

## Questions for next time
- **Did March's sprint come from treating more people or from checking lists?** RTT completed pathways plus removals data (in the full RTT extract, not the kitchen) would show the split.
- **Is Lincolnshire's test backlog slowing its treatment progress later?** Watch whether ULHT's 18-week figure stalls over the next three months.
- **Are the 27 unmatched CQC trusts truly unrated?** Fix the kitchen's merged-trust matching, then recheck finding 6.
- **Cancer waits.** NHS England publishes cancer waiting times monthly (OGL). Adding them would let finding 4 say something about bowel cancer checks safely.
- **Staff Survey trend.** The 2024 survey would turn finding 7 from a snapshot into a change.
