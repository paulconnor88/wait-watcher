# Worked example: NHS waiting times, August 2026

What a full pass looked like, and what Paul's reactions taught us. Two rounds of slides were made; this records which findings landed and why the others didn't.

## Macro pass (what we've got)

- 29 months, April 2024 to August 2026. 566 providers, 24 departments, 36 areas.
- England, August 2026, three figures (waiting / over a year):

  | Figure | Waiting | Over 52 weeks | Use |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All rows of the Full CSV | 7,189,213 | 112,958 | Trusts, departments, areas; shares of England |
  | Commissioner basis ("reported only") | 7,158,164 | 109,936 | **The England figure Wait Watcher quotes** |
  | NHS England headline (with estimates) | 7,370,100 | 113,339 | Name it only when the press is using it |

  The gap between the first two is NONC (31,049 waiting; 3,022 over a year): patients at English hospitals not commissioned by an English ICB, mostly Welsh patients at border hospitals. The headline adds NHS England's estimates for trusts that didn't submit. Reconciled to the unit for every month. Medians matched NHS England's published figures for June and July.
- Trend: improving. 60.9% to 65.2% within 18 weeks in a year; over-a-year waits down 41%.
- Expectation written down first: "things are getting slightly worse". The data disagreed. That became finding 1.

## What was odd

- Royal Berkshire: 3.8-week typical wait for bones-and-joints operations. Too good. Excluded as a likely recording quirk.
- Midlands Partnership and Birmingham Community topped the league at 90%+. Mental-health and community trusts. Excluded from league and most-improved; provider types added to the kitchen.
- 63 independent providers with 15-point monthly jumps. Flagged "check before using".
- A community trust appeared as the fastest place for a colonoscopy. The data wasn't wrong; it was answering a question nobody asked. Rule added: exclude providers with tiny national share of a department.

## Round one: four slides, one survived

| Finding | Landed? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Same hospital, same month: still waiting 14 weeks, treated 48" | No | Needs the two-flavours concept explained first. Homework. |
| Dumbbell chart, six departments, still-waiting vs treated | No | Six comparisons, abstract measure, no place. |
| London bones-and-joints operations: 16 to 48 weeks by trust | **Yes** | A place, one comparison, "about 8 months", no homework. |
| 65 in 100 vs the 92 aim, plus the 18-week right | Partly | Useful as scene-setting, not as a finding. |

Paul's words: "the only one that was interesting enough, and simple enough to understand as a lay person."

## Round two: the rule applied

| Finding | Landed? | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essex 1 in 20 over a year vs next door in east London 1 in 90 | Yes, after a fix | First draft compared Essex with Cornwall. Paul: "Cornwall is far away and not as populous." Neighbours beat distant contrasts. |
| Fastest NHS eye operations in London are private clinics, and free | Yes | Actionable: people can ask for them. Footnote says they take simpler cases. |
| 113,000 waited over a year, "about everyone in Cheltenham" | Yes | Human unit did the work. Weakest of the three: no place. |

## Round three: the monthly format

Paul asked for "most and least improving by area, trust and treatment", calling it "a more interesting angle than random stats". This became the monthly shape:

1. **The good news** (over-a-year waits down 41%).
2. **The turnaround** (Countess of Chester and Mid Cheshire, neighbours, the two biggest improvements in England). The one NHS insiders will share.
3. **The worst spot** (Mid and South Essex holds 1 in 9 of England's year-long waits). System story, not staff story.
4. **The postcode lottery** (oral surgery: 7 weeks at Frimley, 72 at Mid and South Essex).
5. **Did you know** (the 18-week right).

Plus a scene-setter that runs first every month: 18 weeks, 92 in 100, last met 2016, 65 reached, 92 due 2029.

## Lessons to carry

- Good news is under-reported and lands well. Look for it.
- Neighbours, not extremes.
- "Turnaround" beats "best": the question "what did they do?" starts conversations.
- Two slides pointing at one trust reads as a campaign. Spread the worst-spot findings across months.
- A finding Paul can't repeat in the pub after one read is not finished.
