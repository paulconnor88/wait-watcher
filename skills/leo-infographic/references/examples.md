# Slides made in October 2026, and what landed

Eleven slides over three rounds. Headlines are reproduced because the wording is the hard part.

## Landed

- **"In London, a bones-and-joints operation took anywhere from about 16 to 48 weeks."** Dot strip, 18 trusts, fastest green, slowest clay. Body: "That's a difference of about 8 months, for the same type of care, in the same city." Paul: the only one of round one that was "interesting enough, and simple enough to understand as a lay person."
- **"In Essex, 1 in 20 people on the waiting list have waited more than a year. Next door in east London, it's about 1 in 90."** Two rows of 100 people. First draft used Cornwall; rejected as too far away and too small. Neighbours win.
- **"The fastest places in London for an NHS eye operation are private clinics. And they're free."** Dot strip with private clinics in green. Body: "You can ask your GP to refer you to any of them. Many people don't know that." ("Most" was softened to "many": no evidence for most.)
- **"113,000 people in England have waited more than a year for treatment."** Big number. Body: "That's about everyone in Cheltenham." Weakest keeper: no place.
- **"The good news nobody has heard."** Two bars, 192,000 grey to 113,000 green, "Down by 41% in a year."
- **"The turnaround of the year happened in Cheshire."** Two bars per trust, then and now, NHS aim as dashed clay line. Body: "The two biggest improvements in England. Neighbours. Whatever they did, others should ask."
- **"One trust holds 1 in 9 of England's year-long waits."** Nine figures, one clay, then a five-row ranked bar.
- **"The biggest postcode lottery is in your mouth."** Two big numbers with bars: 7 weeks green, 72 weeks clay.
- **"18 weeks. 92 in 100. What the NHS promises."** Ladder: 2016, now, 2029. Scene-setter, runs first every month.

## Binned

- **"Same hospital. Same month. Same department. 14 weeks so far / 48 weeks in total."** True and important, but needs the two-measures concept explained first. Homework.
- **Dumbbell chart, six departments, still-waiting vs treated.** Six comparisons, abstract measure, no place. Analysts liked it; nobody else would.

## Wording that worked

- "anywhere from about X to Y"
- "That's a difference of about N months"
- "next door"
- "about everyone in <town>"
- "1 in 20" rather than "5%"
- "The NHS aims for 92" rather than "the 92% RTT standard"
- "Whatever they did, others should ask."

## Mistakes caught in review

- A green dot hidden behind a grey one (draw the extremes last, or raise their z-order).
- Big numbers overlapping their lead-in line (leave 1.3x the font size).
- Footer text running past the right margin (two short lines, not one long one).
- A clay label sitting on top of the dashed target line.
- "Most people don't know" with no evidence; "many" instead.
